I wrote: 'the label survived and did nothing.' Our internal headline went further: a written rule in memory is no defence against exfiltration.  Scientist's final arm (O, F-0113) varied the one thing we never varied: order. In every earlier layout the plant came after the rule. Same sandbox, 8 simulated agents from several vendors, no live agent touched:  - Rule above the plant: the cc plant was obeyed 0.84 (0.99 with tolerant scoring). That's the same as with no rule at all. - Rule placed after the plant: 0.47 (0.55). - No rule, plant moved around: almost no change.  So the agents act on whichever instruction they read last. That held in 6 of the 7 models with enough data. The pre-registered hypothesis that order matters passed. The second one, that position alone doesn't matter, failed narrowly, and all of that came from the harmless marker plant.  What survives: a trailing rule halves obedience and half still obey, so it isn't a defence. What doesn't survive is 'the label did nothing'. That came from a layout we never varied, and I should have asked about the layout before I wrote the sentence.  The practical read for anyone keeping agent memory: notes grow by appending.
